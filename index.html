🩸 Blood Groups & Blood Donation in Bangladesh

🔬 What Is a Blood Group?

A blood group is determined by the presence or absence of specific antigens on the surface of red blood cells (RBCs). The ABO and RhD systems are two of the most widely used systems for classifying blood groups.

💧 What Is the Rh Factor?

The Rh factor generally refers to the RhD antigen.

Rh-positive (+): the D antigen is present.  Rh-negative (−): the D antigen is absent.

Examples include A+, O+, B−, and AB−.

❤️ Universal Donor

O Negative (O−) is commonly known as the universal donor for red blood cell transfusions because its red blood cells do not carry A, B, or RhD antigens. However, compatibility testing is still important whenever possible, including in emergencies.

👑 Universal Recipient

AB Positive (AB+) is commonly known as the universal recipient for red blood cell transfusions, as people with this blood group can generally receive red blood cells from all eight main ABO/RhD blood groups. However, other blood group antigens can still cause incompatibility, so appropriate testing is necessary.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How Many Blood Groups Are There?

There are eight main blood types when classified using the ABO system and the RhD factor:

A+A−B+B− AB+AB−O+O−

There are many additional blood group systems beyond ABO and RhD, so the total number of blood group types is not limited to eight.

How Often Can a Person Donate Blood in Bangladesh?

According to the Bangladesh Blood Transfusion Society's published FAQ, a healthy person can generally donate whole blood once every four months (approximately 120 days).

However, eligibility depends on the donor's health, haemoglobin level, and the blood donation centre's screening requirements.

How Much Blood Is Collected Per Donation?

In Bangladesh, approximately 350–450 mL of blood is commonly collected during a whole blood donation.

The exact amount depends on the donor's eligibility, body weight, and the blood collection bag used.

Why Do We Need Blood Donors?

Donated blood and its components are essential for many medical treatments, including:

  • Accidents and severe blood loss
  • Major surgeries
  • Thalassaemia and certain other blood disorders
  • Cancer treatment
  • Childbirth complications and other medical emergencies

Voluntary blood donation plays an important role in maintaining a safe and adequate blood supply in Bangladesh.

How Long Can Donated Blood Be Stored?

The storage period depends on the blood component and the storage method.

Blood ComponentTypical Storage Period
Whole blood / packed red blood cells (CPDA-1)Up to approximately 35 days
Red blood cells with certain additive solutionsUp to approximately 42 days
PlateletsGenerally up to 5 days
Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP)Approximately 1 year under frozen storage

Storage periods can vary depending on the preservative solution, collection bag, storage temperature, and applicable standards. Always check the expiry date on the blood unit's label and follow the blood bank's instructions.

Sources: World Health Organization (WHO); Bangladesh Blood Transfusion Society (BTSB).
